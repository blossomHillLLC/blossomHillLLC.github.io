How to Get the CADI or BI Waiver in Washington County, MN

The Community Access for Disability Inclusion (CADI) waiver and the Brain Injury (BI) waiver are Minnesota Medical Assistance programs that can pay for care in the community, including services in a licensed assisted living home. Here is how families in Washington County usually get started.

Who the waivers are for

CADI waiver

The CADI waiver pays for home and community-based services for children and adults with disabilities who would otherwise need the level of care given in a nursing facility. The person must be eligible for Medical Assistance (MA), Minnesota's Medicaid program.

Brain Injury (BI) waiver

The BI waiver is for people with a documented traumatic or acquired brain injury, or a degenerative disease where cognitive problems are present, as long as the condition is not congenital. The person must have significant behavioral and cognitive problems related to the injury and need the level of care given in a specialized nursing facility or neurobehavioral hospital, and must be eligible for Medical Assistance.

Turning 65? People on the CADI waiver may choose to stay on CADI or move to the Elderly Waiver (EW) when they turn 65.

Step by step in Washington County

  1. Request a MnCHOICES assessment. Call Washington County's Access, Aging and Disability intake line at 651-430-6422 (Monday to Friday, 8:00 am to 4:30 pm) or fill out the county's online MnCHOICES referral form. Anyone living in Minnesota can ask for an assessment, and the assessment is free.
  2. Meet with a certified assessor. The assessor talks with the person (and family, if they wish) about their needs, goals and daily support. The MnCHOICES assessment decides eligibility for long-term services such as the CADI and BI waivers.
  3. Apply for Medical Assistance if needed. Waiver services can only be paid once the person is eligible for Medical Assistance. The county can explain the application and the income and asset rules.
  4. Work with a case manager on a support plan. Once eligible, a case manager helps choose services and providers. Assisted living care paid by a waiver is called customized living (see What Is Customized Living?).
  5. Choose a home and tour it. Visit the homes you are considering and ask questions. Our free touring checklist below can help.

Free help in Minnesota

  • Washington County intake: 651-430-6422
  • Disability Hub MN (questions about disability services and benefits): 866-333-2466
  • Minnesota Aging Pathways, formerly the Senior LinkAge Line (help for older adults and caregivers): 800-333-2433
